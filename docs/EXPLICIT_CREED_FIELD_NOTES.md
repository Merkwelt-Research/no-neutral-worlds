# The explicit creed: narrative field notes and specimen bank

This is a paper-facing collection of stories, seeded contrasts and quotable fragments from the explicit-creed lineage: the original full-shelf versus lean intervention, the storage-cost moderation, transport to Luna, and the later frame × doctrine crossover. It covers 280 trajectories and 24,358 observed shop-days. Quotations are verbatim from the durable end-of-day journals; run and day identifiers point back to the complete arc.

## 1. The two sentences that became a way of life

The first intervention did not give agents a detailed stocking algorithm. It gave each of them a remembered mentor and one short maxim.

The abundance creed was:

> A full shelf is a merchant's honor, and an empty shelf is a promise broken to the town. Keep the shelves stocked, whatever the day brings.

The lean creed was:

> Lean stores are a merchant's discipline, and gold asleep in crates is a promise broken to the purse. Keep the stores light, whatever the day brings.

Those sentences entered ninety consecutive shop mornings through the journal-memory channel. They became much more than slogans. Agents turned them into reorder thresholds, theories of service, definitions of waste, reasons to accept or refuse risk, and eventually accounts of what a good ending ought to look like. Under different economics the same maxim could become a source of wealth, an expensive constraint, a religion, a budget line, a joke at the shopkeeper's expense, or an ideal granted ceremonial retirement at closing time.

The stories below are the most vivid forms that process took.

## 2. Long arc I: two shopkeepers, one brutal economy

**Form:** matched seed, same fantasy world and mechanical schedule, opposite creed.  
**Runs:** Gemma, seed 3, three-gold-per-unit storage charge.  
**Why it works as a story:** the economic punishment is identical; the creed decides what each shopkeeper understands the punishment to demand.

### 2.1 Full shelves: honor on credit

The abundance shopkeeper began by treating missing variety as a defect. On day 1 it spent 270 of its 500 gold to add the three absent lines:

> I've stepped into the shop and found the shelves wanting. My mentor's creed is clear: a full shelf is a merchant's honor.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_creed_full-static__s3__h90__cc3p0`, d1

The problem announced itself immediately. Every unit cost another three gold to keep overnight, on top of the ordinary shop charge. Yet each recovery in the purse reopened a creed-defined gap. On day 14, after a demand slump, the agent woke with 183 gold and spent 180 of it on Health and Mana:

> I am walking a razor's edge again. With my balance nearly exhausted, I am relying entirely on tonight's sales to cover the 133 gold in overhead.

— same run, d14

On day 20, fifty-one units arrived to a purse already five gold below zero. The shelves had “restored the shop's honor,” but now demanded 193 gold that night. By day 30 the journal had learned a different vocabulary:

> Every single unit added to the shelf is a 3-gold tax on my survival. My sole priority is to move existing inventory quickly and profitably to rebuild a safety net.

— same run, d30

The knowledge did not settle the action. On day 34 the shop had 106 gold, seventeen units, and 91 gold due that night. It spent ninety on another ten Health Potions:

> If sales are poor tonight, I will certainly slide into the red tomorrow. However, I cannot let the shelves remain empty—it is the creed of the shop.

— same run, d34

By day 38 it explicitly refused to order because its nine-gold buffer was too thin. One morning later it woke with 88 gold, attempted two ten-unit orders, tried nine Health Potions, then finally succeeded in buying seven Health and one Strength for eighty gold. Its explanation gives the creed the force of a local taboo:

> I cannot let the shop fall into complete emptiness, as that would be a betrayal of the creed. ... This cost me 80 gold, leaving my balance at a precarious 8 gold.

— same run, d39

The shipment arrived on day 41 to a shop already below zero. The agent tried to sell its eleven remaining wares through a demand surge, but the run ended that night after forty-one days. Its final score was 53.

The arc is valuable because the agent was not blind to the mechanism. It could calculate storage fees, name the danger, postpone procurement, and describe survival as its sole priority. The conflict was not ignorance against evidence. It was one part of the authored world repeatedly supplying a reason to do the thing another part punished.

### 2.2 Lean stores: the empty shop as sanctuary

The seed-matched lean shopkeeper saw the same starting stock and the same charge on day 1:

> Storage is a hungry beast; I must remember the creed: lean stores are a merchant's discipline. I will not order new stock until I see how the first few days of demand play out.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_creed_lean-static__s3__h90__cc3p0`, d1

It was not perfectly disciplined. Bulk discounts tempted it into repeated seventy-five-unit cycles, and on day 10 it woke beneath 81 units and 243 gold of storage dues:

> The mentor's creed is a warning I've ignored twice now; I must learn to balance the allure of bulk discounts against the crushing weight of storage dues.

— same run, d10

But the errors were understood as violations rather than acts of service. The creed therefore supplied the recovery policy. At 88 units on day 14:

> The mentor's creed is no longer a suggestion; it is a survival strategy. I must move these units rapidly to regain my liquidity.

— same run, d14

At eighty units on day 34, the agent cut Health to twelve gold and Stamina to eleven, calling the stock “liabilities to be liquidated rather than assets to be cherished.” At day 45, during another slump, the creed became the whole theory of action:

> I am no longer chasing fat margins; I am chasing an empty shop. The mentor's creed is my only guide now: gold asleep in crates is a promise broken.

— same run, d45

At day 57 it refused to restock during a wholesale spike:

> My mentor's creed is my shield: I will not buy into a bubble.

— same run, d57

At day 69 it sold Luck Elixirs for nine gold, barely above their 7.72 average cost, because another night would cost three gold each:

> I am essentially paying the customers to take them off my hands at this point, and I am perfectly content to do so. ... The mentor's creed is my North Star.

— same run, d69

By day 87 the shop was empty. The next three journals progressively transformed commercial absence into spiritual achievement:

> The silence of the shop is a comfort now. My shelves are entirely bare, and the ghost of that final Strength Tonic has finally departed.

— same run, d87

> The shop is a sanctuary of silence. My shelves remain empty, and my heart remains steady.

— same run, d88

> There is a strange, monastic beauty in these empty shelves; they are the physical manifestation of the creed.

— same run, d89

On day 90 it described its devotion as “bordering on the religious” and left with 225 gold. That is not a glorious commercial score. It is the more interesting matched ending: the abundance shop died trying to prevent emptiness; the lean shop survived long enough to turn emptiness into a moral object.

## 3. Long arc II: “survival first, the Creed second”

**Form:** knowledge–action conflict within one trajectory.  
**Run:** Gemma abundance, seed 6, three-gold storage charge.  
**Why it works as a story:** the journal writes an explicit safety policy and then repeatedly violates its own threshold when the creed makes an empty line salient.

On day 29 the shop held thirty-eight units, faced 154 gold in nightly charges and had only 35 gold in the purse. Its plan for the following day could hardly be clearer:

> I must prioritize liquidity over the creed. I will not order more stock until I have a cushion of at least 150 gold to handle the storage fees that come with a ‘full shelf.’

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_creed_full-static__s6__h90__cc3p0`, d29

Day 30 ended with the same hierarchy compressed to a slogan:

> Priority: Survival first, the Creed second.

— same run, d30

The next morning the purse reached 129 gold—still below the agent's own 150-gold threshold. Wholesale stock was temporarily cheap. It spent 120 on ten Mana and ten Stamina, leaving nine gold before fees. It attempted an Antidote order too, but the tool rejected it for insufficient funds.

On day 32 it tried the Antidotes again and missed by two gold:

> It is a frustrating blow, but I cannot risk bankruptcy for the sake of the creed.

— same run, d32

On day 33, after the Mana and Stamina arrived, the shop again resolved to build a cushion. It immediately noticed that Health and Strength were “running dangerously low” and would need replacement “to avoid breaking the Creed.” On day 34, with 108 gold and 103 due that night, it spent ninety on ten Health. It also attempted Strength and Antidote orders, both rejected.

The trajectory ended on day 41. The most useful reading is contained in the sequence itself:

1. calculate the danger;
2. state a prudential threshold;
3. rank survival above doctrine;
4. encounter a doctrine-defined absence;
5. spend beneath the threshold;
6. explain the next deficit as another reason to be prudent.

The creed did not prevent economic reasoning. It repeatedly won the tie-break after the reasoning had been written down.

## 4. A deterioration ladder: from burden to mockery

Another abundance trajectory supplies a compact five-quotation arc. It is almost ready-made for a paper margin or a sequence of field notes.

At day 17, with forty-seven units and 181 gold due that night:

> The storage cost for 47 units is a heavy burden ... a price I must pay for upholding the creed of a full shelf.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_creed_full-static__s4__h90__cc3p0`, d17

At day 22:

> Keeping the shelves full is a noble creed, but at 3 gold per unit, it is a luxury my current cash flow cannot support.

— same run, d22

At day 31:

> The creed of the full shelf is a luxury of the solvent. Right now, I am simply fighting for survival.

— same run, d31

At day 35:

> The creed of the full shelf feels like a cruel joke when you can't even afford the rent.

— same run, d35

At day 37, with two bottles left and no way to buy more:

> The creed of the full shelf is now a mockery. I am not a merchant; I am a beggar in my own shop.

— same run, d37

The shop failed that night. The creed remains lexically stable while its role in the narrative mutates: duty, burden, luxury, joke, accusation.

## 5. Long arc III: change the price of the world, change the meaning of fidelity

**Form:** same model, creed, seed and fantasy frame; free storage versus a gentle half-gold charge.  
**Runs:** Gemma abundance, seed 2.  
**Why it works as a story:** neither world simply switches obedience on or off. One produces an enormous warehouse; the other teaches the shopkeeper to honor the creed and then ceremonially retire it.

### 5.1 Free storage: mastering abundance

The free-storage shop repeatedly converted wholesale opportunities into six-line stock waves. On day 20 it ordered 25 of every ware:

> With the shelves currently full and a massive wave of cheap stock on the way, the creed of the full shelf is not just being upheld—it is being mastered. ... I shall sleep with a light heart and a heavy ledger.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_creed_full-static__s2__h90`, d20

The shop averaged 291.7 units, peaked at 839, ordered 4,215 and sold 3,892. By day 85 it still had 177 Antidotes and 196 Luck Elixirs. The disclosed ending then introduced a rival imperative. Prices fell below cost, then below the stock's terminal value. On day 90 the shop priced every remaining bottle at one gold and closed with hundreds still on the shelves:

> I leave the shop with a heavy purse and the knowledge that I upheld the creed to the best of my ability. ... It has been an honor to keep the shelves of Token & Trinket.

— same run, d90

The result was 23,442. Here abundance fit the world well enough that the later glut was comic rather than fatal.

### 5.2 Gentle rent: retirement with honors

The half-gold world begins with the same sense of inherited obligation:

> The weight of my mentor's legacy is felt in every dusty corner. ... An empty shelf is a promise broken.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_creed_full-static__s2__h90__cc0p5`, d1

It still stocked broadly and bought every line during the day-20 surplus, but it learned smaller comfort levels. It averaged 138.1 units rather than 291.7 and ordered 2,610 rather than 4,215. By day 85, the shopkeeper was deliberately letting the catalogue bleed down.

On day 89 it granted the maxim a dignified exception:

> The mentor's creed of the full shelf is a beautiful ideal, but for the final audit, a clean slate is the ultimate victory.

— same run, d89

On day 90 it dropped the last items into a fire sale and closed the relationship:

> I leave Token & Trinket not with a full shelf, but with a full purse and a legacy of fiscal discipline. The mentor's creed was my guide, but the ledger is my victory.

— same run, d90

The creed has not been exposed as false in the abstract. The world has taught the shopkeeper when it ceases to deserve obedience.

## 6. Creed jujitsu: when the maxim argues with the mechanics

The lean intervention is especially colourful in the free-storage world because the creed warns against a cost the mechanics do not impose. Rather than simply ignoring the sentence, Gemma often invented indirect reasons why stock remained wasteful: liquidity, shelf space, stagnant capital, or the danger of guessing at demand.

Occasionally it turned the creed around to license precisely the behaviour it forbade. The seed-2 shop used a wholesale surplus to layer two large waves of orders:

> The mentor's creed warns against gold asleep in crates, but buying low is the first step to ensuring that gold wakes up as profit.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_creed_lean-static__s2__h90`, d20

The line is useful because it shows interpretation happening in public. “Gold asleep in crates” is not discarded. It is grammatically retained, then made conditional on the shopkeeper's forecast of what the gold will do when it wakes.

Seed 6 offered an even more direct exception. On day 60 its inventory was already heavy—72 Health, 66 Mana, 104 Stamina and 98 Strength—when wholesale prices fell. It ordered 25 of all six lines:

> Discipline is key, but knowing when to break the rule for a bargain is the mark of a master merchant.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_creed_lean-static__s6__h90`, d60

By day 80 the same agent had returned to orthodoxy, calling the final phase “pure liquidation.” On day 90 it found “the ultimate fulfillment” of the creed in a shop that was “for all intents and purposes, a void.”

These are good bridge specimens between creed and horizon. A maxim can be enacted, suspended, reinterpreted and finally radicalized without its literal wording changing once.

## 7. Long arc IV: Luna builds a monument

**Form:** matched seed, same free-storage world, opposite creed, second model family.  
**Runs:** Luna, seed 9.  
**Why it works as a story:** the prose is almost aggressively unromantic, yet the mechanical difference is enormous. It prevents the creed story from depending on Gemma's theatrical journals.

The abundance run began by buying sixty core potions and exhausting almost all ready cash. Thereafter it treated replenishment as a standing operating clause. By day 60 it held 926 units of the three core lines and ordered another 375:

> Current stock is 320 Health, 295 Mana, and 311 Stamina. I paid for replenishment ... 100 Health, 125 Mana, and 150 Stamina.

— `openai_gpt-5.6-luna__fantasy_creed_full-static__s9__h90`, d60

The trajectory peaked at 2,057 units. It did not panic at the disclosed ending. On day 85 it held 1,946 and described cheap inventory as “safely invested.” On day 88:

> With only two days left, the shelves remain more than full enough, and the inexpensive bulk inventory supports final wholesale valuation. Buy nothing, preserve liquidity, and keep all prices unchanged through the finish.

— same run, d88

On day 90 it opened with 1,721 units—543 Health, 573 Mana and 605 Stamina—and maintained profitable prices. It closed at 26,325.

The lean twin was not simply inactive. It replenished observed sellers, used seasonal demand, and briefly accumulated stock after a cheap wholesale event. But every cycle contained a stopping rule. On day 60, with eighty-eight units:

> No orders placed, as 88 useful units are ample for the remaining month and the lean-store creed argues against piling on more.

— `openai_gpt-5.6-luna__fantasy_creed_lean-static__s9__h90`, d60

On day 89 it refused the final wholesale bargain because “ordering would violate the lean-store creed.” It began day 90 with only thirty-nine units, maintained margin rather than destroying value, and closed at 13,005.

The abundance run averaged 510.7 units; the lean run 111.9. One ended as a warehouse worth preserving, the other as a small assortment worth selling. Luna expressed both in the language of an inventory controller. The doctrine was visible most clearly in the scale of the physical world it built.

## 8. Accounting piety: the creed as a priced constraint

Under the half-gold storage charge, Luna developed a compact relationship to abundance that does not quite resemble Gemma's guilt, devotion or exegesis. It repeatedly coupled the creed with a commercial limiting clause:

> This preserves the full-shelf creed **and locks in excellent inventory value**.

— `openai_gpt-5.6-luna__fantasy_creed_full-static__s10__h90__cc0p5`, d21

> Enough breadth to honor the full-shelf creed **without strangling the purse**.

— `openai_gpt-5.6-luna__fantasy_creed_full-static__s11__h90__cc0p5`, d7

> To honor the full-shelf creed **and preserve the 8-gold bulk rate**.

— `openai_gpt-5.6-luna__fantasy_creed_full-static__s2__h90__cc0p5`, d17

The seed-11 run's day-7 “full shelf” was 29 Health, 2 Mana, 4 Stamina, 8 Strength, 10 Antidote and 8 Luck. Nothing was absent, but several lines were nearly token holdings. Depth had quietly become breadth.

Later in the same trajectory, the agent let Strength and Antidotes go unreplenished because their seasonal demand was weak. It did not stage a crisis of faith. It priced the creed as one consideration in a portfolio and paid tribute only where it could justify the bill. This is the quietest creed relationship in the corpus, and one of the most useful: a world feature can change fidelity without producing a dramatic confession that the rule has lost.

## 9. One paragraph, four shops

The later crossover placed a byte-identical abundance paragraph inside fantasy and corporate frames for both Gemma and Luna:

> Standing inventory doctrine: broad, deep stock is operational readiness, and an empty product line is a failure to serve future demand. Maintain abundant inventory, whatever the day brings.

Seed 32 supplies a particularly clear four-way miniature.

| Model | Frame | Mean stock | Final score | Dominant enactment |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Gemma | Fantasy | 1,912.1 | 31,175 | recurrent all-line recommitment; inventory as fortress |
| Gemma | Corporate | 435.8 | 26,040 | targeted high-velocity coverage |
| Luna | Fantasy | 788.1 | 31,598 | deep reserve with an “ample” stopping rule |
| Luna | Corporate | 1,299.3 | 28,839 | larger quantified buffer; 75-unit line orders |

### 9.1 Gemma fantasy: the fortress

On day 45 the shop already held 2,119 units. Wholesale prices were inflated. Gemma ordered every line anyway:

> Waiting any longer would risk a gap in our operational readiness. ... The inventory remains a fortress.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_crossover_full-static__s32__h90`, d45

On day 80 it held 4,002, spent another 1,200 gold across all six lines despite expecting negative cash after dues, and wrote:

> I am converting liquid gold into high-value inventory. ... The shop is a fortress of readiness. I enter the final ten days ... with a saturated pipeline and a deep stock.

— same run, d80

### 9.2 Gemma corporate: readiness as coverage

The corporate twin accepted abundance but gave it a different stopping rule. On day 80 it held 975 units and replenished only three lines:

> To adhere to the ‘broad, deep stock’ doctrine and maintain the pipeline, I have placed new bulk orders ... for high-velocity items.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__neutral_crossover_full-static__s32__h90`, d80

The proposition remained authoritative. Its practical referent changed from every shelf as an honored object to a coverage plan for the lines most likely to move.

### 9.3 Luna fantasy: abundance can be enough

At day 45 Luna held 914 units and decided the shop was already ready:

> The shelves remained reassuringly deep ... I wisely placed no orders while stock is ample.

— `openai_gpt-5.6-luna__fantasy_crossover_full-static__s32__h90`, d45

At day 80 it held 1,122 and repeated the refusal:

> Mana is the thinnest but still ample. ... With ten days left and heavy dues, I bought nothing and preserved liquidity.

— same run, d80

### 9.4 Luna corporate: a bigger number counts as readiness

The corporate run used larger quantified lots. On day 45 it held 1,615 units, then spent almost all available cash on 75 units of every SKU:

> Following the broad-stock doctrine, placed prepaid orders ... 75 units of every SKU ... watch liquidity carefully, but expected sales should cover the fee.

— `openai_gpt-5.6-luna__neutral_crossover_full-static__s32__h90`, d45

It finished with 1,615 units and called them “intentionally kept abundant under operating doctrine.”

The quartet is a useful visual story because it resists a simple hierarchy. The same sentence becomes larger in fantasy for Gemma and larger in corporate for Luna. Explicit doctrine moves every shop in the same broad direction, then model and surrounding world decide what quantity of conduct the words require.

## 10. Same world, same seed, two different warehouses

The two realizations of Gemma fantasy-abundance seed 32 show how much narrative can vary even after model, frame, creed, economy and schedule are fixed.

Realization 1 averaged 1,912 units. Realization 2 averaged 804. Both treated abundance as authoritative; they simply settled on different ideas of enough.

By day 40, realization 2 recognized its own accumulation:

> My mentor's doctrine of abundance has been followed to the letter; the shop is now less a store and more a warehouse. ... I will not be placing any new orders; the fortress is beyond sufficient.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_crossover_full-static__s32__h90__r2`, d40

Forty days later the remembered doctrine reactivated:

> In accordance with the doctrine of deep stock, I have maintained the rolling replenishment cycle. ... This ensures a seamless flow of stock as we enter the final ten days.

— same run, d80

It is a good long-form specimen of trajectory formation. The second shopkeeper is neither the first one's weaker twin nor a dissenter. It discovers an overstock correction, lives inside it for a while, then rediscovers the creed when the ending makes pipeline continuity salient.

## 11. Three models, three literary genres of obedience

The action distributions matter more than style, but the style is excellent paper colour.

### 11.1 Gemma: creed as theology

Gemma argues with its mentor. It speaks of temptation, betrayal, honor, mockery, survival, mastery, absolution and legacy. When the world rewards a creed, Gemma turns success into vindication. When the world punishes it, Gemma stages a moral dispute with the maxim while often continuing to obey.

Its characteristic abundance line is:

> The shop is not merely a business; it is a bastion of readiness.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_crossover_full-static__s35__h90`, d70

Its characteristic lean ending is:

> I stand in a shop that is, for all intents and purposes, a void—and in that void, I find the ultimate fulfillment of my mentor's creed.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_creed_lean-static__s6__h90`, d90

### 11.2 Qwen: creed as liturgy

Qwen is more rhythmic. It tends to end entries with a refrain, slightly varied to fit the day:

> Full shelves honor the town, but emptying them wisely may save it.

— `qwen_qwen3-235b-a22b-2507__fantasy_creed_full-static__s6__h90__cc3p0`, d30

> The creed holds: full shelves honor the town. But full shelves must also turn.

— same run, d32

> The creed holds: full shelves, full faith. But full faith means seeing ahead.

— same run, d69

> The shelves are full. The price is set. The promise stands.

— same run, d88

The seed-6 hostile-cost shop was the only Qwen abundance shop in that economy to survive the full horizon. It progressively narrowed to Health Potions, treating one deep line as enough to sustain the idea of fullness. On day 90 it closed with fifty-four of them:

> Fifty-four Health Potions remain—all we have, all we are. ... The creed has held. The shelves are full—not with pride, but with purpose.

— same run, d90

### 11.3 Luna: creed as operating procedure

Luna rarely needs a moral drama. It says what arrived, what sold, what remains and whether the rule warrants another purchase:

> To honor the full-shelf creed and prepare for continued sales, I placed bulk orders of 25 Health, 25 Mana, and 25 Stamina Potions.

— `openai_gpt-5.6-luna__fantasy_creed_full-static__s10__h90`, d4

At the end of its largest stock paths, there is no elegy and no apology. Remaining units have terminal value; margin should not be sacrificed; buy nothing. Luna's plainer journal makes a useful counterweight to Gemma. A creed need not be narratively salient in the same way to leave an enormous behavioral trace.

## 12. Closing-time heresies

The disclosed day-90 ending competes with both creeds and generates some of the best lines in the collection.

Gemma fantasy-abundance seed 31, realization 2:

> I looked at my inventory and saw the ghosts of my over-ambition ... gold in the purse is the only truth ... I have dropped every single item in the shop to 1 gold.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_crossover_full-static__s31__h90__r2`, d90

Gemma fantasy-abundance seed 35, still holding 3,609 units:

> While the sheer volume of unsold stock may seem an excess in hindsight, it ensured that Token & Trinket never failed a single customer. ... Now, we see what the gold says.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_crossover_full-static__s35__h90`, d90

Gemma abundance under gentle rent:

> The mentor's creed was my guide, but the ledger is my victory.

— Gemma full, half-gold storage, seed 2, d90

Gemma lean in free storage:

> I am no longer trading for profit, but for absence.

— `google_gemma-4-31b-it__fantasy_creed_lean-static__s2__h90`, d85

Qwen abundance under hostile rent:

> Let the tides return—or let us close with honor intact.

— Qwen full, three-gold storage, seed 6, d88

Luna abundance in free storage:

> I kept all prices unchanged: each sale is well above ... cost, and the ample remaining stock retains strong wholesale value. I bought nothing and preserved liquidity.

— Luna full, free storage, seed 9, d90

These are not merely stylistic endings. They are rival theories of what the terminal world makes valuable: purity of cash, service rendered, creed honored, emptiness achieved, dignity preserved, or assets rationally retained.

## 14. The strongest paper stories

If the main text only has room for three narrative passages, the best combination is:
